# Patterns — 小镇喧嚣

Recurring governance and negotiation patterns. Each entry states when it appears, the mechanism, the risk, and a book example.

## 1. 迎检生产链 (Inspection Production Chain)
**When** a higher-level inspection carries 一票否决 or ranking pressure.
**Mechanism** Mobilize all staff → select a model site → produce 台账/表格/数据 → coach households on what to say → manage the visiting route → follow-up banquets and gifts.
**Risk** The text becomes the reality; actual service gaps are hidden and reproduced.
**Example** Family-planning inspection: cadres prepare accounts, cards, and registers, then “impressionize” object households so they can answer questions about follow-up visits (Ch.1, Ch.14).

## 2. 层层加码 (Target Ratcheting)
**When** higher levels set a quota and lower levels compete for recognition.
**Mechanism** Each layer adds a margin “to be safe”; the top’s “reasonable” target becomes the bottom’s impossible one.
**Risk** Padding, 垫税, falsification, and resentment; the last layer bears the cost.
**Example** Tax tasks and 国地税 “提前过关” through 协税, 引税, and cadres' own money (Ch.10).

## 3. 擂—媒交替 (Pressure + Particularism)
**When** formal command alone no longer produces compliance.
**Mechanism** Publicly press with deadlines and rankings; privately invest in meals, face, and favors; turn the public task into a personal obligation.
**Risk** Compliance depends on persons, not rules; rotation of cadres breaks the machine.
**Example** Township leaders “媒” village cadres into signing compensation agreements, then use them to pressure relatives (Ch.6).

## 4. 一把钥匙开一把锁 (One Key per Lock)
**When** a group has formed a 攻守同盟 or collective refusal.
**Mechanism** Identify each member's weak point—history, violations, family ties, party membership, side business—and offer a tailored private deal.
**Risk** Rewards the most obstructive; destroys collective trust and predicts future holdouts.
**Example** Four-group holdouts in 洲头 are peeled off one by one using 刘力民's history, 于兴明's illegal construction, and 于祖文's party status (Ch.6, Ch.11).

## 5. 公事私化 (Public Task → Private Favor)
**When** the state needs village cadres to do something unpopular.
**Mechanism** Build friendship, give face, lend money, attend weddings; then ask for help “as a friend.” Refusal becomes a moral debt.
**Risk** Erodes universal standards; village cadres become personal retainers.
**Example** 乡镇干部 cultivate 村干部 through meals and favors so that tax collection and land deals become personal obligations (Ch.6, Ch.15).

## 6. 打包式了结 (Lump-Sum Settlement)
**When** multiple claims are entangled and item-by-item adjudication is costly or impossible.
**Mechanism** Suspend fault-finding; aggregate claims into a total; exchange a payment or concession for a signed closure; leave secondary issues for 善后.
**Risk** Hides responsibility; invites new claims once the lump sum is known.
**Example** 叶桦's investment claim is compressed into a negotiated package through agent 贺志伟; 塘堰 and 土方 claims are traded in one deal (Ch.11).

## 7. 代理人骑墙 (Broker Double-Dealing)
**When** an outside investor or a village needs representation before government.
**Mechanism** The agent claims to represent the principal while signaling loyalty to government; each side bids for the agent's favor; the principal's bottom line leaks downward.
**Risk** The principal is under-compensated; the agent captures the difference.
**Example** 贺志伟 moves 叶桦's claim from 3 million to 870,000 while promising to “assist government work” (Ch.8, Ch.11).

## 8. 弱者的武器 → 反弱为强 (Everyday Resistance → Situational Leverage)
**When** coercion is costly and the state needs cooperation.
**Mechanism** Delay, non-cooperation, 种房, 违建, 阻工, 缠闹, petitioning; each raises the ruler's cost without directly challenging sovereignty.
**Risk** Escalation into violence or a hardened “刁民” identity; public order becomes privatized.
**Example** Villagers build extra floors before expropriation; holdouts block bulldozers; 石场 owners petition Beijing (Ch.7, Ch.8, Ch.12).

## 9. 耗油 (Exhaustion)
**When** the opponent has higher time and money costs than the government.
**Mechanism** Delay responses, refuse substantive meetings, stop hospitality, buy hard-seat tickets, wait them out.
**Risk** Short-term quiet, long-term distrust; filters out only the poor, not the determined.
**Example** 石场 owners' second Beijing petition is met with cold treatment; they run out of money and split (Ch.12, Ch.13).

## 10. 行政经济化 (Administrative Economization)
**When** superiors transfer a lump sum with “超支不补、节余自留.”
**Mechanism** Every layer becomes a contractor: bargain down the price, retain a margin, subcontract, pay in favors.
**Risk** Public funds become private rents; the weakest layer absorbs the cut.
**Example** 西气东输 复耕经费 is contracted down from the construction company to towns, villages, groups, and farmers (Ch.12).

## 11. 诱民致富 → 风险回流 (Induced Development → Risk Backflow)
**When** government promotes a crop or project with subsidies and guarantees.
**Mechanism** Promise inputs, technology, and sales; farmers contribute land and labor; when markets or techniques fail, farmers demand compensation from the organizer.
**Risk** “政府搞一次农调就失败一次”; fiscal and legitimacy losses; successful pilots are kept as showcases.
**Example** 大陈村 藠头 succeeds and is kept for seed; 前王村 fails completely and farmers demand 300 yuan per mu (Ch.4, Ch.14).

## 12. 依法明是非，按乡情了事 (Law for Fault-Finding, Custom for Closure)
**When** a dispute has a clear legal answer but enforcement would destroy long-term relations.
**Mechanism** State the legal position first; then broker a compromise in which each side gives up a little; frame it as 乡情 and face.
**Risk** Legal rights become bargaining chips; the visibly stubborn gain.
**Example** 李国辉's land claim: the 经营权证 favors the current holder, but other households surrender parcels to restore peace (Ch.13).

## 13. 灰色中介 (Gray Brokerage)
**When** formal enforcement is unavailable or too costly.
**Mechanism** A local figure with rough reputation and networks takes over a contract, clears a blockage, or “persuades” holdouts; officials acquiesce.
**Risk** Public authority is franchised; costs rise; violence becomes a governance tool.
**Example** 陈伟业, a “道上的伢们,” takes the 潭水湾 dredging contract at a price far above the official estimate (Ch.13).
