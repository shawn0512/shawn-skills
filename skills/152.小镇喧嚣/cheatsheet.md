# Cheatsheet — 小镇喧嚣

Fast decision rules for reading or operating in grassroots Chinese governance. Use as hypotheses, not laws.

## Diagnosing a Situation

- **If a task is backed by 一票否决 or ranking → expect 迎检 behavior**, not routine service.
- **If policy looks good on paper but fails in the field → check 激励结构**: who is rewarded, who bears risk, who can veto.
- **If a cadre is pushing a project → ask “whose 政绩 is this?”** Visibility to superiors often outweighs local benefit.
- **If a project is a “试点” → expect resource concentration**; success may not replicate without the same attention.
- **If a village is described as “难搞” → look for a history of broken promises**, not innate culture.
- **If everyone agrees publicly but nothing happens → look for private side deals** and 一把钥匙开一把锁.

## Predicting Behavior

- **When coercion is politically costly → the strong will “讨要,” not command.** Expect supplication, favors, and delay.
- **When the state needs village cooperation → it will “媒” the village cadre**, turning public tasks into personal debts.
- **When a group forms an alliance → expect 分化瓦解**: each member has a weak point or a price.
- **When a holdout is public and noisy → expect 点发**: a private concession to quiet them, denied to others.
- **When a contract is signed → expect 合同随形势变**; “形势” is a renegotiation clause.
- **When land value rises → expect 争地**: former leavers return, contracts are challenged, rights are relitigated.
- **When compensation is negotiated → expect a lump sum**, not itemized justice; 善后 will reopen later.
- **When an opponent petitions → calculate 耗油**: who can wait longer? Time is a weapon.
- **When an inspection approaches → expect text production and coached households**, not new services.

## Negotiating

- **Open with the rule; close with 乡情.** Legal clarity establishes the baseline; relational concession makes closure possible.
- **Never humiliate a counterpart who must keep face in the village.** Leave a ladder down.
- **Price in the cost of enforcement**, not just the nominal claim. A cheap settlement may be cheaper than a correct victory.
- **Identify the real decision-maker.** The titled leader may be less important than the broker, the relative, or the上级.
- **Separate the person from the demand.** Attack the claim's feasibility, not the claimant's dignity.
- **Use deadlines and rankings publicly; use meals and favors privately.** Mixing them destroys both.
- **Expect reversals after signing.** Build in delay, witnesses, or partial delivery to manage 反悔.
- **Do not create precedents you cannot generalize.** A private concession today becomes tomorrow's collective demand.

## Risk and Ethics

- **Beware “花钱买平安.”** It buys quiet, not legitimacy, and teaches others to escalate.
- **Beware “扶强不扶弱.”** It produces showcase successes and invisible failures; ask who is absorbing the risk.
- **Beware “行政经济化.”** When cadres become contractors, public funds become rents.
- **Beware “不出事逻辑.”** Avoiding incidents can mean avoiding the problem.
- **Beware the “刁民” label.** It moralizes a bargaining position created by institutional gaps.
- **Beware legalism without enforcement capacity.** A judgment no one can execute may worsen the dispute.

## Post-Agricultural-Tax Era

- **The binding constraint is no longer extraction but project acquisition.** Cadres compete for 项目 and 资金 from higher levels.
- **“服务” is rhetorically central but weakly rewarded**; visible projects and revenue still drive careers.
- **Village cadres' brokerage role intensifies** as they mediate between projects, contractors, and villagers.
- **Inspection does not disappear when the tax does**; it migrates to family planning, stability, and project performance.

## Quick Diagnostic Questions

1. Who assigned the task, and how is it measured?
2. Who bears the cost if it fails?
3. What can the weakest party do to raise the cost of enforcement?
4. Which relationships, not which rules, will decide the outcome?
5. What private concession is available to break a coalition?
6. What will reopen after the current deal is signed?
7. Is the goal compliance, legitimacy, or merely quiet?
